What the calculator measures

A two-motor planar linkage: the carrier drives the knee, the crank drives the connecting rod, and the lower leg carries a pickup and a foot. Both motor shafts are fixed to the leg assembly. The third, shoulder servo is held at its CAD-neutral orientation here.

The foot is an idealised point. Installed link lengths and shaft frames come from the repository; the Part 027 outline, rocking contact, boots, joint clearances, servo housings and collisions are not simulated. A bent lower leg can be represented with the foot-angle control. “Body clearance” is measured from the exported chassis hull bottom to the requested ground.

Torque is calculated from virtual work, τ = −JᵀF, with the entered external foot force. It excludes link weight, acceleration, friction, compliance and motor losses. It is required support torque, not available servo torque. Equal supporting-leg loads and the displayed centre of mass are assumptions. The side view is not a support-polygon or fall prediction.

Servo travel reports excursion over this sample path. It does not verify absolute horn indexing, pulse limits, real end stops or loaded speed. Results with missing samples are partial. Near-toggle configurations can have very high or undefined torque. There is no universal best ratio: compare reach, height, excursion, load and mount changes for the same task.
